## Why two levels, not one audit for everyone

Most audits sold in the market are Level 1 in substance, sold at Level 2 prices: a list of software, with commentary. That is useful, but it stops at the stack. **Pseudo-consulting stops at the stack.** The real cost of an underperforming organisation rarely lives only in tool choice — it lives in how work moves between people.

So we split the offer honestly instead of folding one into the other:

Level 2 is never forced. Many clients get everything they need from Level 1 — a smaller company running four SaaS tools does not need an organisational value-chain study. Level 2 earns its price when the organisation is dense enough that process friction outweighs the software bill.

## The NDA trigger, explained

We are precise about when confidentiality escalates, because it should not be a blanket clause that makes every engagement feel heavier than it is.

- **Level 1** touches software licences, subscription costs, and technical configuration — information a vendor invoice or an admin panel already shows. A lighter confidentiality clause in the quote covers this, unless you ask for more.
- **Level 2** touches how your organisation actually operates — internal processes, management data, sometimes financial elements tied to margin or headcount. That is materially more sensitive, so a signed **NDA always precedes any Level 2 access**, no exceptions, before a single interview or document review begins.

If you start at Level 1 and later decide to extend to Level 2, the NDA is signed at that point — you are never asked to sign it upfront for work you have not yet committed to.

## How the three-year TCO figure is built

The **Total Cost of Ownership** projection is the number most clients actually forward to their CFO, so it is worth explaining what goes into it rather than leaving it as a black box:

- **Current-state cost**: every subscription, licence, and per-seat fee at today's rate, projected forward assuming no changes — including typical vendor price increases where a pattern exists.
- **Hidden cost**: integration maintenance, workarounds, and the operational cost of vendor lock-in when a tool becomes harder to leave than to keep.
- **Alternative-state cost**: what a self-hosted or owned equivalent would cost to build and maintain over the same three years, including our own estimate for the work involved — not a marketing number.

The comparison is **indicative and built per client** during the audit itself, from your actual invoices and tool list — never a generic industry average dressed up as your number.
