# Neurobiologie de quand le travail est achevé

Part of the series [Satisfaction when work is done](/research/publications/series/satisfaction-when-work-is-done/).

When a task ends — not “paused”, not “handed off”, but **finished** — the nervous system runs a different script. That moment is [[dopaminergic-closure|dopaminergic closure]]: the prediction-error loop settles, tension drops, and a clean boundary appears between “open loop” and “done”.

## Why this matters outside the lab

Product teams optimise for engagement loops. Ops teams optimise for throughput. Both often **deny the organism a completion signal**. The result is ambient unfinished work: tabs, tickets, half-ships, perpetual “almost”.

Designing for [[task-completion|task completion]] is not soft psychology. It is systems hygiene:

- smaller ship units so closure can fire more often
- explicit “done” states (not only “in progress” and “blocked”)
- recovery windows after hard closes, not more stimuli
