# Partner fees and commercial terms

Numerimondes works with advisory firms, specialist organizations and independent professionals when their expertise or client relationships fit a defined opportunity. The right commercial arrangement depends on who sells the work, who delivers it and which entity contracts with the client.

This page explains the main ways a collaboration can be structured. It is a guide, not a partnership offer, employment offer, commission schedule or contract. A collaboration starts only after the parties agree its scope and commercial terms in writing.

### <span class='silky-bbi'>Accounting and advisory firms</span> {#firms}

Accounting and fiduciary firms often see operational problems early: recurring manual work, unclear reporting, disconnected systems or difficulty hiring. A firm may introduce a client who wants to discuss one of these needs. The client decides whether to make the introduction, and any referral fee, commission or delivery role must be agreed with the relevant parties in writing before it is earned.

Possible client needs include an operational diagnostic, business software, automation, infrastructure, recruitment or a scoped improvement project. An introduction does not authorize the sharing of confidential client information or create an automatic right to a commission.

### <span class='silky-bbi'>Specialist firms</span> {#specialists}

Consultancies, studios and other specialist firms may contribute expertise, capacity or a local service line. Depending on the opportunity, the firm can deliver work under its own client agreement, take a defined subcontract, or provide a service that Numerimondes introduces to a client.

For each assignment, the parties agree who owns the client relationship, who contracts and invoices, what each party delivers, the acceptance process, the price and how changes are handled.

### <span class='silky-bbi'>Freelancers and independent specialists</span> {#freelancers}

Independent consultants, developers, designers, recruiters, trainers and operators may deliver defined work or sell their own services through an agreed referral route. A fee may be set for a project, milestone, day, hour or recurring capacity, depending on the work.

The specialist remains responsible for their own business, taxes and professional obligations. A collaboration does not by itself create employment, exclusivity or authority to bind Numerimondes or another partner.

## <span class='silky-bbi+115'>Ways a partner can earn</span> {#commercial-models}

| Commercial route | How the money flows | What must be agreed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Your own services** | You sell, deliver and invoice your own service to your client. You set your own price and receive the client payment under your own agreement. | The service, price, client ownership and any referral or network fee. A fee is due only if the relevant written agreement says so. |
| **Specialist delivery** | You deliver a defined part of a client project for the entity that contracts that work. That paying entity pays your agreed fee against your invoice or other required business document. | Deliverables, rate or fixed fee, acceptance, changes, invoicing, payment timing, currency and expenses. |
| **Sales commission** | A commission may be paid for an eligible sale by the entity named in the deal-specific arrangement. | Product line, paying entity, eligible deal, commission basis and rate, payment trigger, currency, timing, transfer costs and how any later rate change applies to open opportunities. |
| **Introduction or referral** | A referral may lead to a paid role, a commission or no payment, depending on the written terms. | Whether the introduction qualifies, who follows up, how the outcome is recorded and whether compensation applies. |

No commission percentage or universal partner rate is published here. Before a commission-based sale begins, the parties should confirm the applicable rate and calculation in a written schedule for the relevant product line and paying entity.

If a rate or delivery fee depends on changing costs, volume or other measurable inputs, the schedule should identify the data source, review frequency, approval owner and effective date. Changes should follow the agreed process and should not be assumed to apply retroactively to an existing opportunity.

## <span class='silky-bbi+115'>Shared terms, defined before a deal</span>

| Term | Meaning for a partner discussion |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Potential partner** | A person or organization considering collaboration. A discussion or introduction alone does not make someone a partner or create a right to payment. |
| **Partner** | An independent person or organization working with Numerimondes under an active written agreement for the relevant collaboration. |
| **Network** | Numerimondes and the partners or projects involved in a particular opportunity. Each party keeps its own business and responsibilities. |
| **Product line** | A defined family of offers, such as recruitment, software or advertising. The product line determines which offer and commercial schedule apply. |
| **Your own services** | Services you deliver and invoice in your own name, under your own client agreement. |
| **Paying entity** | The company or partner organization that owes a delivery fee or commission under the agreement for a particular deal. |
| **Closed deal** | The written agreement should define when a sale qualifies. A proposed definition is a client contract signed and the corresponding client payment received by the paying entity. |
| **Commission** | A percentage or amount payable for an eligible deal, with its basis, rate, payer and payment trigger stated in writing. |

The terms used in a partner proposal should not be assumed to apply to every partner, country, service or transaction. The deal-specific written agreement and schedule control.

The service catalogue and capabilities library describe client needs and offer families. They do not automatically define partner eligibility or commission categories.

The partner framework proposal groups network sales into software, advertising, import and export, and recruitment. The broader catalogue also includes advisory, infrastructure, people and operations services. The product line for a particular deal must be identified in its written terms; do not infer it from a capability article or catalogue category.

## <span class='silky-bbi+115'>What to settle before starting</span>

| Before work or sales begin | Agree in writing |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Whether you are introducing, selling, closing, delivering or supporting the client. |
| **Client and data** | Who owns the client relationship, who may contact the client and how client information may be used. |
| **Scope and quality** | Deliverables, responsibilities, acceptance, handover and any support after delivery. |
| **Commercials** | Fee or commission basis, payer, invoice requirements, payment date, currency, expenses and transfer costs. |
| **Changes and exit** | How scope changes, ongoing work, client transitions and earned amounts are handled. |

Local business registration, invoicing, tax treatment and cross-border payment requirements depend on the parties and the transaction. Each party should confirm its own obligations with an appropriate local professional.
