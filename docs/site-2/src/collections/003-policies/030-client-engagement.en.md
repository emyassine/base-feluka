# Client engagement policy

We take on client work when the need, expected work and responsibilities can be described clearly enough to propose. A conversation, capability page or catalogue listing is not a commitment to deliver a service.

## Before work starts

A written proposal or agreement defines the work. Depending on the assignment, it identifies the intended outcome, scope, deliverables, exclusions, price, payment terms, schedule, client inputs, acceptance and any support after delivery. Work begins after the relevant parties accept those terms and any stated start conditions are met.

We do not treat an estimate, discussion or marketplace example as a confirmed price or delivery commitment. The [Pricing policy](/policies/pricing) explains how we price work; each proposal confirms the terms for that engagement.

## Scope changes and decisions

Work outside the agreed scope requires review and written agreement before it is treated as part of the assignment. Changes may affect price, timing, deliverables or responsibilities. The proposal or agreement defines how acceptance, feedback and change decisions are handled.

We do not promise a business, financial, recruitment, search-ranking or other outcome that depends on factors outside the agreed work. We describe intended outcomes and assumptions, then report what the work produced.

## What we do not assume

Unless the written terms say otherwise, a client engagement does not include unlimited revisions, open-ended availability, ownership or licensing beyond the agreed terms, ongoing hosting or maintenance, or a guarantee that third-party services will remain available. Cancellation, refunds, expenses, intellectual property and dispute handling must be stated in the engagement terms for the assignment.

We do not begin unscoped work on the assumption that the details can be settled later.
